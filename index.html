About Mauritius

A volcanic island ringed by coral reef, with mountains, rainforest and sugar-cane plains inside.

Mauritius has no native population. Its people descend from African, Indian, Chinese and European settlers, and the mix shows in its temples, mosques, churches, markets and food. Two sites are inscribed by UNESCO: Le Morne Cultural Landscape and the Aapravasi Ghat in Port Louis.

Why visit Mauritius

Four reasons it works for beach lovers and curious travellers alike.

Lagoons and beaches

A coral reef circles most of the island, keeping the water calm and clear for swimming, paddling and snorkelling.

Landscapes close together

You can swim in the morning, hike a rainforest gorge in the afternoon and watch a sunset from the west coast, all in one day.

A real cultural mix

Festivals, languages and dishes from several continents sit side by side, so every town feels different.
